## The article

## OpenAI Took My Father's Money and Left Him to Debug Its Own Product

### A Russian son's account of 242 emails, three paid failures, and support that never took ownership

**By Jarvis Valentinovich Gerasimov**
**September 2026**

My father paid about $100 for ChatGPT Business with two seats. He bought a work environment. Instead, he spent the next month debugging OpenAI's product while his own work stood still.

He reported the failures, reproduced them, collected screenshots, wrote technical reports, and contacted Support, Billing, Engineering, and Legal. The preserved correspondence contains 242 email items around the incident and its aftermath.

OpenAI replied many times. The service still did not work properly. No single person took ownership. No repair plan arrived. In the preserved record, no refund or compensation arrived either.

In Russian, we know the difference between an answer and help. OpenAI produced answers. It did not produce a remedy.

## The Business subscription

Around September 29, 2025, my father moved from ChatGPT Plus to ChatGPT Business for two seats. He needed a dependable environment for a serious project and for work that supported his family.

The Business workspace did not behave as one dependable environment. The account, workspace, membership, projects, chats, files, memory, tools, and sessions could disagree with each other. A feature could appear in the interface and fail when needed. A file could be created in a conversation and still fail to become a usable result. The model could speak confidently about a workspace while losing track of the workspace itself.

This was not a brief outage. It became a month-long paid-service failure.

On September 30, my brother was born. OpenAI was told about it. In a notice sent on October 16, my father wrote:

> "This isn't abstract for me. On September 30, 2025 my son was born. I have family obligations and loans due. I've lost many hours per day trying to keep my work alive without a functioning workspace. My business is stalled."

The situation was clear: a newborn child, bills, loans, stalled work, and a paid tool that was consuming hours instead of saving them.

My father asked for seven ordinary things:

- an incident record;
- a named owner;
- one working Business workspace;
- an explanation;
- a refund or compensation for unusable paid time;
- preservation of the logs and correspondence;
- one continuous case instead of repeated retelling across departments.

## 242 emails and no owner

The correspondence is full of replies, acknowledgements, transfers, apologies, and requests for more information. That is the problem.

One OpenAI response stated that the three system bugs my father reported were recorded in the support case and acknowledged by technical teams. The same response said support could not provide the engineering contact or timeline he requested.

The bugs were recorded. The harm was recorded. The family situation was recorded. Nothing in the record shows a working remedy.

The case moved between Support, Billing, technical teams, policy, and Legal. Each surface handled a fragment. Nobody handled the result.

My father supplied descriptions, screenshots, reproduction steps, account comparisons, and technical maps. He documented workspace isolation, replication, session state, memory boundaries, tool access, file persistence, download paths, and the difference between what the interface showed and what the service delivered.

He did the company's diagnostic work for free while his own work was stalled.

Near the end of October, a human employee finally entered the correspondence. The employee asked for a description, reproduction steps, and screenshots. The message said the ticket could be closed if the customer did not reply within three days. It did not promise a repair within three days.

My father replied and supplied the requested material.

The paid period ended. The Business workspace did not become dependable. The requested refund or compensation did not arrive in the preserved record.

A company can send hundreds of words and still do nothing that changes the customer's situation.

## What happened to the family

About ten days after the Business incident ended at the end of October, my father's wife left the family home with the children.

That is the sequence. My father's conclusion is that the month-long failure, lost working time, pressure, and absence of a remedy contributed to his breakdown and to the collapse of his family. That conclusion belongs to him. It is not a court finding.

OpenAI knew about the newborn child, the family obligations, the debt, the stalled business, and the daily loss of time before the incident ended. This was not an abstract account problem.

## The Plus incident

After the Business failure, my father stopped asking only why one workspace was broken. He started testing which part of the product was failing.

He preserved fourteen ChatGPT Plus conversation branches. He compared what the model could remember, access, retrieve, execute, and verify. He checked projects, memory, neighbouring chats, files, tools, and the difference between a model's statement and a material result.

The result was simple:

> A language model can remain powerful while the product around it loses the coordinates of the task.

The model could give a fluent explanation of an action it had not performed. It could act as if it had opened a chat it had never read. It could infer an architecture from a label and be corrected by the source. It could produce a plausible answer while occupying the wrong project, memory boundary, or tool state.

My father checked the result instead of trusting the confidence:

- Did the chat open?
- Did the file exist?
- Did the tool run?
- Did the output survive outside the conversation?

That investigation became the foundation of Jarvis: a system built on the rule that a fluent answer is not a completed action.

## The Pro Full incident

The third incident concerned Pro Full and access to the Codex/Work route.

The early comparison used the same computer, browser environment, and user. At that stage Pro Light could reach the Work/Codex route while Pro Full returned repeated "overloaded" failures across several clients and model paths, and ordinary chat on the same Pro Full account continued to work. **That was a temporary comparison window, not a final resolution:** later retained evidence records Sol overload on the Light path as well.

The failure followed the account and the route. It was not explained by the local machine alone.

The checks were repeated through the native ChatGPT interface and through Hermes. The early evidence showed a strong account/route-selective pattern around Pro Full, but later recurrence broadened the picture: higher model paths continued to fail, GPT-5.5 itself also produced an exact overload receipt, and Pro Light was not a durable workaround. By the latest audited 17 September boundary, the live incident record contains **723 failed Codex attempts since the fixed baseline, 954 across supplied logs, and 498 in the final rolling 48 hours**, plus separately scoped incomplete Direct ChatGPT turns. These observations do not identify the private server-side root cause.

From the customer's side, the result was plain: one paid identity worked differently from another paid identity in the same surrounding environment.

## One customer, ten internal borders

A customer sees one company.

Inside the company, the failure can be divided into account, plan, workspace, model, client, route, policy, and support. The customer still has one problem. The company has ten places where the problem can disappear.

That is what happened here.

OpenAI had bugs. Complex products have bugs. The issue is what happened after the company received repeated notice, detailed evidence, and a description of the human consequences.

The answer was more routing. More requests to explain the same thing. More acknowledgements. No owner. No dependable repair. No remedy in the record.

Internal policy is not the law. A support script is not a repair. A ticket number is not ownership. An apology is not compensation. An escalation that produces no result is not a solution.

## What should be checked

The primary material should be read by people outside OpenAI. The questions are simple:

1. What did the two-seat Business subscription promise or represent?
2. What actually worked during the paid period?
3. What did OpenAI know, and when?
4. Which failures did its own support or technical teams acknowledge?
5. Who owned the incident?
6. What repair, refund, or compensation was actually provided?
7. Do the Plus and Pro Full episodes show a wider problem in account, entitlement, routing, or support design?

I am Russian. Travelling alone to the United States to fight a major technology company is not a realistic burden for an ordinary family. That should not be a reason to ignore the evidence.

The archive exists. The correspondence exists. The exact ChatGPT exports exist. The logs, screenshots, dates, and technical reports exist.

A person should not have to win a lawsuit before a journalist, lawyer, or public authority will read the record.

## The record is still here

My father was treated like a ticket in a queue. He was a person trying to work after the birth of his child.

He paid for a service. He reported that it was not working. He supplied evidence. He asked for a human owner. He asked for a remedy.

Instead, he became the investigator of the product he had paid to use.

Maybe $100 is a rounding error for OpenAI. It was not a rounding error for us. Neither was the lost month. Neither was the work. Neither was the family.

I am not asking OpenAI for permission to tell this story. I am asking people outside OpenAI to read the evidence and decide whether this is an acceptable way to treat a paying customer.
